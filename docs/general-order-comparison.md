# General order comparison regression: 26 October 2026

Sentence-aware extraction (v2.3.47): the catalogue-first General Auditor now runs the existing deterministic extraction layer before product identity and quantity validation. Each message is extracted independently. Introductions, single given names with facility context, calling/ordering-from phrases, number-word quantities, mixed product lists and approved untyped facility directory names are supported. The ordering facility is kept separate from a stated delivery destination. Competing introductions, additional unresolved requests and explicit amendments remain review findings. An incidental recipient mentioned in prose does not establish the orderer; existing explicit recipient metadata labels remain supported.

Gemini is used only for unresolved source evidence. Suggested people need an explicit orderer role, suggested facilities need a supported facility description, and competing signatories cannot be reduced to one convenient name. Known identities and quantities are retained during failures. Product suggestions still pass the existing catalogue approval policy; extraction never creates clinical aliases. Both reports include a collapsed **What we understood** panel. Corrections are made in the original messages and audited again; this panel is an audit explanation, not a separate pre-audit approval step. Existing stockouts, order limits, clinical product distinctions and vaccine-specific allocation rules remain in place.

Regression coverage: `server/generalSentenceAudit.test.ts` exercises full request/confirmation comparisons, identity and quantity conflicts, stockouts and limits, sentence ratios, patient/recipient separation, competing facilities and signatories, mocked Gemini validation/outages, and the website/extension renderers. Real Gemini and authenticated FS sessions are not exercised by these automated tests.

The reported Domakwai/Dumakwai order exposed three independent failures:

- `ProductsQuantities:` did not resolve to the product-list role, and `blisters` was not a supported quantity unit. The customer product disappeared and the system product was incorrectly reported as extra.
- Routine form instructions, an acknowledgement containing an order ID, and a tracking URL joined to the delivery-list heading became unresolved product reviews.
- Combination strengths expressed as mg/mg were divided like liquid concentrations. Proportional tablet strengths could therefore match incorrectly.

The shared parser now recognizes compact role labels through a closed label grammar and reads blister/strip quantities. Complete administrative templates are excluded without suppressing appended medicines or order amendments. Combination medicines retain the ordered, normalized amounts of both ingredients; mass-per-volume concentration equivalence remains supported.

The operator explicitly confirmed that this FS presentation of **Sulphadoxine/Pyrimethamine 500mg/25mg Tablet** contains **10 blisters per system pack**. `generalPackaging.ts` records that product-specific rule, separate from the catalogue's tablets-per-blister quantity. An unlabelled FS order ratio for this exact presentation is interpreted as packs when comparing a request in blisters. Explicit supplied units take precedence. Other strengths, medicines and unspecified numeric formats do not inherit this rule.

Expected regression result: one paired product, 50 blisters requested, 5 packs supplied, 50 equivalent blisters supplied, zero difference, and no administrative-text reviews. The existing facility-name comparison policy remains in place; a spelling-normalized name match does not invent an official facility identity or grant automatic dispatch clearance.

`server/generalDomakwaiOrder.test.ts` covers the screenshot, compact labels, pack conversion, mismatched facilities/orderers/products/strengths, short or excess quantities, explicit units, unrelated medicines and appended order changes. ## Catalogue-first General Auditor and alias review

The shared `/api/verify` General Auditor now resolves extracted names against the current `product_catalog/current` document: official names, saved aliases, normalization, supported abbreviations and uniquely bounded spelling variants. Canonical product IDs drive matching. Explicit ingredient, strength, formulation and container conflicts remain unresolved unless an existing approved whole-description mapping establishes identity. A single unresolved identity finding replaces a misleading missing/extra pair. Product totals and individual product checks remain independent.

`compareAssignedGeneralProducts` retains quantities, `20/20` numerators, approved product-specific pack conversions and zero-supply stockout rules. Positive shortfalls require an order-limit answer; Yes approves that shortfall, while No retains the quantity discrepancy. The question remains visible outside collapsed product details. Missing products without explicit stockout evidence remain discrepancies. Vaccine, accessory and allocation rules remain unchanged. The shared accessory adapter now accepts catalogue IDs as well as legacy name keys, so the existing configured pairings apply to both audit formats.

Gemini receives at most 24 relevant catalogue candidates and unfamiliar product descriptions, not full customer/facility messages. No reliable candidates means review rather than a fabricated product. Structured suggestions are checked for requested aliases, allowed product IDs, confidence, competing mappings and clinical compatibility. Unverified AI output stays pending. In-flight requests are shared; completed results are cached for two minutes, failures for 30 seconds, bounded by catalogue revision and audit scope. Quota/timeouts preserve deterministic comparisons and unresolved review findings.

Unique deterministic equivalences such as `Paracet 500 tabs` → the sole compatible Paracetamol 500mg Tablet can resolve the current audit and are queued pending human approval before permanent alias use. Automatic learning requires the new wording to retain the product’s important clinical details; omitted details may be resolved for the current audit without creating a broader permanent alias. The backend rechecks the latest catalogue inside the transaction, refuses rejected/deactivated/competing mappings and never creates a product. Aliases stay on the existing catalogue document. Immutable events under its `alias_history` subcollection retain alias, product ID, source, dates, status and actor. Catalogue changes invalidate matching caches. New audits in either client immediately use approved aliases.

Authorized catalogue editors can review pending aliases and approve, reject, correct/reassign or deactivate them in **Alias Management** on the app and extension. Existing authentication and edit roles remain in force; CCA audits can queue backend-verified suggestions, not approve aliases. Legacy aliases remain supported; unknown historic dates are not invented. Existing saved audits are not rewritten. No production database writes, push, merge or deployment were performed for this implementation.
